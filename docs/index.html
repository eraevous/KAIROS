We are building Cognitive Augmentation.

The project's guiding light, in the owner's words. Its full definition is being written by the owner and will arrive with the papers. Until then every repo here is judged by one question: does it increase a person's capacity to realise their intentions?

Kairos is Zach Rhodes's umbrella for his AI/cognition work: a set of tools, the operating environment that coordinates the people and AI agents who build them, and the research behind both.

Install the Dev Kit →

Ten minutes, one script, one folder. Download kairos.py, run python kairos.py install, and you have the four public repos, the two kits, a private records store of your own and its Cockpit page. No administrator rights, no account; nothing leaves your machine. Uninstall by deleting the folder.

What this is for

Powerful AI tools let one person do work that used to need a team.

The systems coordinating that work usually hide:

  • what was decided,
  • why, and on what evidence,
  • whether a later change quietly broke an earlier conclusion.

Kairos is an attempt to keep those things attached to the work, in records a stranger can audit later, and to find out, by measurement rather than assertion, whether doing so makes a person more capable or just busier.

Three ideas recur in every repo

  • The reason stays attached to the thing. Purposes, requirements, claims, decisions and objections are records that link to code and to each other, append-only.
  • Information crosses freely; authority never crosses implicitly. Agents read everything and propose anything. Only the owner accepts.
  • Count and ask; never fabricate meaning. Every claim carries how it was established (measured, inferred, declared) and tools refuse to compute what the data cannot support.
